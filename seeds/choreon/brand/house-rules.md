# Choreon rules (on top of the studio rules)

Choreon's films sell the studio by being the proof: a motion designer watching should think "every frame of this was decided". Precision is the spectacle; the critic treats any violation of the "Banned" list as **blocking**.

## Banned (immediate rejection)
- Generic "AI" imagery: robots, brains, sparkles, glowing orbs, circuits, chat bubbles saying "thinking…".
- Glow, neon, halo, drop shadow, lens flare, chromatic aberration, added grain, gradients of any kind.
- Fake or invented UI or features: everything shown exists in Choreon (brief, framing, storyboard, shots, critique, notes, timeline, sound, UI kits, lessons) and is rebuilt from the real app.
- Opacity fade as the only entrance or exit; cross-dissolves.
- Empty "corporate" calm: a slow pan over a near-empty frame, a lone centered title that merely appears, a 1.5 s window where nothing changes before the final lockup.
- Soft-body physics: jelly, wobble, squash and stretch, bouncy text or panels. Overshoot only with `springs.pop` on keyframe hits.
- A timecode, frame counter or score that is wrong for the instant shown.

## Allowed and encouraged (wins over any studio lesson that says otherwise)
- **Hard cuts on the beat**, smash cuts, whip pans with heavy motion blur, match cuts between shapes (diamond → playhead → `o`).
- **Giant Geist type** filling or overflowing the frame, cropped by the edges, cut in on the beat.
- **Split screens** and grids of frames (contact sheets, film strips), night ↔ paper full-frame inversions, up to 2 inversions per second on a beat (never sustained strobing).
- **Frame counters, timecodes and scores** ticking at the frame rate; annotation boxes drawn on a frame in `signal`.
- **The real Choreon UI** rebuilt as components and animated: panels slide on a grid, cards snap, the playhead scrubs.

## Structure
- The final frame is the one still moment: the lockup and the tagline, held ≥ 1.5 s.

## Sound
- Tight and percussive, synced to the frame: clicks, ticks, typewriter keys, shutter and tape-transport sounds, hits on cuts. Music with a clear beat drives the edit; one beat of silence before the logo. Commercially licensed music only.
