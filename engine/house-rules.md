# Studio rules (every project, every style)

Craft rules that hold whatever the style. They say nothing about taste: physics (overshoot or not), transitions (cuts, wipes, fades), texture, particles, palette, density and pace belong to the project — its doctrine `brand/motion.md` and, when present, its own rules `brand/house-rules.md`. The critic treats any violation of a "Blocking" list — this one or the project's — as a **blocking** defect.

## Blocking (every project)
- **Accidents**: a jump between two frames, a dead frame nobody chose, an element that pops in or out without a gesture, a ghost or a residue (orphan text fragment, empty container, stray dash), an overlap or a crop that the storyboard did not intend.
- **Unreadable message**: the text that carries the message is cropped, covered, too small or too short on screen at the moment it matters.
- **Off-doctrine**: a gesture, a transition or a texture that the project's doctrine or rules exclude (overshoot in a project that bans it, a calm shot in a project that bans calm…).
- **Off-brand**: colours, typefaces or logo outside `brand/` — the logo is built from its layers, as designed.
- **Motion without intent**: every gesture serves the story or the doctrine (reveal, connect, transform, punctuate a beat). Pure decoration only where the doctrine asks for it.

## Product truth
- On-screen text and the features shown really exist in the product (or are given by the brief). Never an invented feature, figure or customer.
- An interface is rebuilt as animatable components (UI kits, cards, fields), not a static full-frame screenshot, unless the brief supplies footage.

## Message and structure
- The lines of the brief's **locked message** are used word for word, with no added slogan.
- The hook reads from the **first second**; the **last frame** is sharp, stable and held at least 1.5 s (unless the brief asks for a loop).

## Timing
- The storyboard's beat map is the contract: each state at its instant (± 0.1 s), each shot's first frame continuous with the previous shot's last.
- Motion blur keeps shapes and letters readable; it never turns into a smear or a ghost.

## Method (gates not to skip)
1. Approved brief (interview) → 2. storyboard with a **beat map** (time, state, action, sound) approved before any code → 3. stills of the key instants looked at before rendering → 4. render → 5. scored critique → fixes.
- A shot is animated only once its storyboard is approved; a touch-up changes the minimum.

## Sound
- Sound effects synced to picture to the frame (the engine aligns the sound's peak with the instant). Commercially licensed music only.

## Project rules
A project adds its own rules in `brand/house-rules.md`: its bans, its permissions, its tone. They come on top of these, settle every question of taste, and win over any studio lesson that contradicts them.
